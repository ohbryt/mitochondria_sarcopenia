# Cover letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “The PPM1K–BCKDK–BCKDH axis in sarcopenia:
convergent multi-omic and preclinical evidence with independent transcriptomic
non-replication,” as an integrative translational analysis.

The manuscript addresses a common target-discovery failure mode: equating a
biologically plausible pathway with a replicated human gene target. We combine
primary evidence from human sarcopenia multi-omics, causal mouse experiments,
skeletal-muscle flux studies, and BCKDK pharmacology with a deterministic audit
of four human muscle case-control subsets. PPM1K, BCKDK, BCKDHA, and BCKDHB do
not replicate individually at the transcript level. We therefore do not claim
a validated target or drug candidate. Instead, we define a falsifiable
activity-first programme requiring p-BCKDHA Ser293 target engagement,
isotope-resolved BCAA oxidation, genetic dependence, mitochondrial safety, and
rescue of donor-level specific force.

We believe the work is valuable because it reconciles apparently conflicting
human evidence, separates pathway causality from transcript abundance, and
shows why BT2 is a mechanistic reference rather than a translational candidate.
All secondary-analysis claims are supplied with exact source rows,
machine-readable status files, and input hashes.

This work reanalysed de-identified public data and published aggregate evidence;
no new human participants or animals were enrolled. The author is affiliated
with Brown Biotech and declares no other competing interests. The work received
Brown Biotech internal support and no external funding.
